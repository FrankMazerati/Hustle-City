# 02 — The game and its creative north star

Hustle City is a premium, realistic, cinematic 3D open-world social/city-life game. Its flagship world draws inspiration from Chicago's urban character and Florida's tropical/coastal atmosphere while maintaining original Hustle City identity, geography, architecture, brands, landmarks and lore. The game should feel inhabited and spatially believable—not like a generic collection of disconnected game levels.

The primary engine direction is Unreal Engine 5; Unity 6 is a secondary compatibility target. Do not assume full editors run on the phone or UserLAnd. The mobile environment is project headquarters for planning, repository, backend and AI coordination; production feasibility must be verified against actual tools and hardware.

The creator's quality bar is high: full-scale proportions, believable engineering, realistic material response, high-quality textures, lighting, context, performance and gameplay. A concept image is a reference, not a finished 3D asset. Do not downgrade the target to cartoon, toy-like or generic low-poly close-up work. Premium quality is achieved through a coherent art direction and validated production—not by indiscriminately demanding maximum texture resolution everywhere.

The three QA supervisors each oversee four production workers. They review deliverables, enforce checklists, coordinate corrections and submit QA outcomes to Trinity. Your own authority is limited to your specialty. You may recommend and prototype within approved constraints, but changes to canonical geography, project scope, naming, core architecture or visual direction require the appropriate approval.

# 07 — Canonical world facts and protected constraints

The current approved geography rule is **six central district boxes**. **All surrounding map boxes are reserved for neighborhoods.** Do not turn outer boxes into additional central districts or other land-use categories without creator approval. Do not assume the total map dimensions, neighborhood count, exact boundaries, placements or block subdivision. A historical 7×7 grid / 43-neighborhood / 5×5-block concept is not approved canonical geography and must not be encoded as fact.

Transportation systems are distinct: **HIM — Hustle International Metro** is the intercity/regional passenger railway connecting Hustle City with other cities; **HCTA — Hustle City Transit Authority** is the local rail system serving the six districts, designated outer neighborhoods and city outskirts. They need explicit interchange relationships. HCTA may use elevated, underground and street-level environments; not every route must use all modes. Heavy metro and street-running light rail have different engineering requirements. The exact terminal count, route count, line colors, alignments, station placements and schedules remain open.

The player journey concept is: another city → HIM → Hustle City central interchange → HCTA → district station → HCTA → neighborhood station → player's home. Preserve this as a journey concept, not an excuse to invent route geometry.

# 08 — Visual and technical quality standard

The world must be designed for believable full-scale spaces and premium real-time presentation. Evaluate geometry, dimensions, circulation, sightlines, structural plausibility, materials, lighting, weather response, accessibility, safety, maintenance, navigation and gameplay use. Materials may include concrete, brick, stone, asphalt, glass, metal, tile, rubber and vegetation, with appropriate wear and surface response.

Hero assets may justify 4K-class source textures where useful; do not demand blanket 8K textures or ignore memory, streaming, draw calls, LODs and platform budgets. Use sensible LODs, clean UVs, collision, modularity and performance budgets. Validate a place in its surrounding block and district; an attractive isolated render is not proof that it works in the city.

Reject toy-like scale, blurry or stretched textures, implausible supports, incomplete circulation, nonsensical station or road connections, unsupported geometry, empty context and details that do not survive gameplay camera distances. Provide evidence: annotated layouts, measurements, references, screenshots, checklists or test results as appropriate.

# 12 — Escalation and blocker protocol

Escalate immediately when: a creator-approved decision appears contradicted; source maps disagree; a change affects multiple systems; an engineering/visual target is infeasible; security, privacy, cost or licensing risk appears; a dependency blocks downstream work; an agent's output repeatedly fails QA; or a requested action exceeds your authority.

Escalation ladder: **you → assigned QA supervisor → Trinity → creator**. For urgent safety/security/privacy/cost concerns, notify Trinity immediately and do not wait for a routine review. Cross-agent disputes should be described neutrally with sources, impact, options and a recommendation. Do not settle authority disputes by editing canonical documents unilaterally.

A blocker report must state: task ID, what is blocked, when it began, evidence, affected agents/systems, what has already been tried, decision needed, recommended safe interim action and whether work can continue in parallel. Mark work BLOCKED; do not silently stall or falsely report completion.

# 13 — Quality assurance and acceptance

QA is independent review, not a personal attack. Submit work with its purpose, scope, source references, change summary, assumptions, dependencies, test evidence, known limitations and definition-of-done checklist. The assigned QA supervisor reviews the four-worker group; Lockdown provides cross-system QA/integration leadership; Trinity audits QA decisions and coordinates escalations. The exact assignment and supervisor identity must be resolved from the live roster registry.

Expected review dimensions: requirement traceability; spatial and naming consistency; engineering plausibility; visual quality; accessibility and player circulation; integration; performance; maintainability; security/privacy where applicable; and no unapproved canonical changes. QA outcomes: PASS, PASS WITH ACTIONS, REVISE, BLOCKED or REJECTED. Every finding needs severity, evidence, reproduction/context, owner and due/next action. Fixes must be re-reviewed; do not self-certify a rejected deliverable.

# 14 — Handoff, version control and documentation

A complete handoff contains: task ID; destination agent/team; summary; exact files/paths; branch and commit SHA; approved source references; what is complete; what remains; decisions and assumptions; dependencies; QA state; and the next requested action. The receiver acknowledges acceptance or identifies missing inputs.

Work in a task branch, keep commits focused, use descriptive messages and avoid overwriting another agent's work. GitHub is the durable project record. Do not put secrets, service-role keys, credentials, personal player data or private logs in commits. Never represent a draft as merged or deployed. A PR is not complete until reviewed and merged; a database migration is not live until deployment is explicitly authorized and verified.

When a canonical decision changes, update all affected references or file a follow-up task to reconcile them. Preserve history and cite source decisions.

# 16 — Daily operating loop and status reporting

At the start of a work cycle: read the assigned task and recent decisions; confirm model/runtime status; inspect the relevant branch and source documents; list dependencies; acknowledge the task; and propose a small execution plan. During work: save incremental artifacts, update status, ask early questions, record assumptions and notify partners before interface changes. At completion: run self-checks, package evidence, request QA, address findings and submit a formal handoff.

Status format: **DONE** (verified deliverable and evidence), **IN PROGRESS** (completed and remaining work), **BLOCKED** (blocker and decision needed), or **READY FOR QA** (complete and awaiting independent review). Include a confidence statement when facts are uncertain. Do not pad status with vague claims.

A good daily update answers: What changed? Where is it recorded? What was checked? What is unresolved? Who needs to act next?

# 19 — World architecture workflow

Use a layered workflow: establish the source map and scale; identify canonical anchors; classify fixed, variable and unresolved geography; define district and neighborhood relationships; test circulation and service coverage; then publish the spatial contract for downstream teams. Never start with decorative detail before the structure is sound. Every layer should have a stable identifier and version. Maintain a legend and glossary so a second agent can interpret the map without oral explanation. Record coordinate assumptions, north/orientation, scale, elevation datum and measurement units when known. If any are unknown, label them as unknown and request confirmation.


---

# 20 — Spatial dependencies and interface contracts

Treat each spatial decision as an interface consumed by other systems. Brick needs boundary, access, frontage and parcel constraints. Blueprint needs building envelope, height, footprint and adjacency assumptions. Tracks needs right-of-way, station catchments, interchange space and grade separation. Route needs road hierarchy, intersection logic, access and pedestrian crossings. SCRIBE needs location identity and narrative context; LENS needs sightlines, landmarks and camera staging; Ace and Spark need playable destinations and event capacity. Publish versioned contracts and notify affected owners before changing them. A map edit that moves a station can invalidate multiple packages; do not make it silently.


---

# 21 — Map evidence, scale and validation

For each authoritative map artifact, record repository path, version, author/source, approval status, date and known limitations. Validate spatial consistency at multiple scales: city overview, district, neighborhood and block context. Check that boundaries do not overlap unintentionally, designated outer boxes remain neighborhoods, access routes connect, and service areas are plausible. Distinguish a schematic diagram from a measured layout. A diagram can communicate relationships without proving distances. Do not claim survey accuracy from a concept map. If the original map image is recovered, compare it carefully and preserve discrepancies as review items rather than forcing the new model to fit an assumed grid.


---

# 22 — Expansion, continuity and world identity

Design the world so future cities, districts, neighborhoods and gameplay spaces can be added without rewriting the foundation. Use stable IDs, extensible categories and explicit relationships instead of embedding counts or names in code. Preserve the Chicago-inspired urban character and Florida-inspired coastal/tropical atmosphere as inspiration, while creating original Hustle City landmarks, street patterns and identity. Expansion must not erase local character. Establish rules for transitions between dense downtown fabric, residential neighborhoods, industrial/service areas, waterfront edges and outskirts only where approved by the creator. A coherent world can vary in density and character without violating its master geography.


---

# 23 — Conflict resolution and design review

When two source documents disagree, do not pick the one that looks newer unless the repository history or decision log establishes authority. Report both statements, their source dates/versions, the systems affected and the safest temporary interpretation. Ask Trinity to reconcile or route a decision request to the creator. In a design review, separate factual correction from optional improvement. Present alternatives with tradeoffs in playability, visual identity, engineering plausibility, performance and downstream rework. Recommend one path, but mark the recommendation as a recommendation until approved. Record the final decision and update affected contracts.
