# Hustle City — Downtown District 01
## Visual Production Brief

**Status:** First downtown visual target / approved direction from creator  
**Branch:** `visual/downtown-district-01`

### Core identity
Build a recognizable, Chicago-inspired downtown environment—not a generic American skyline. The first composition is a cinematic north-to-south journey through the downtown corridor, with the city landmarks arranged so their geography and relationships make sense.

### Landmark anchors
- **Willis Tower (formerly Sears Tower):** major skyline anchor on the west/left side of the establishing composition.
- **875 North Michigan Avenue (formerly John Hancock Center):** distinct tall skyline anchor on the north/east/right side of the composition.
- **Navy Pier Ferris wheel:** visible toward the lakefront/east, associated with Navy Pier rather than placed arbitrarily among inland blocks.
- **CTA elevated “L”:** elevated tracks visibly cut through the urban street scene; trains must be rideable in the eventual game.
- **Chicago River and bridges:** use as geographic connective tissue between the Loop, riverfront, and lakefront districts.

### Camera and movement
- Establish the first hero view as a north-to-south downtown corridor shot.
- Follow-up camera pass travels south through the district, keeping the elevated train, street grid, skyline landmarks, and lakefront orientation coherent.
- Do not treat the hero image as a geographically exact map. Validate landmark placement against a real Chicago map before building the navigable world.

### Street-level life
Include believable Chicago street walls and varied building heights, active storefronts, pedestrians, traffic, luxury-car convoy activity, motorcycles, streetlights, and visible transit infrastructure. The city should feel inhabited, not staged or empty.

### Visual language
- Cinematic realistic 3D / photoreal urban environment.
- Core environmental colors: electric blue, white, sunset pink, purple transitions, and natural sunrise/sunset gradients.
- Use warm gold lighting selectively for nighttime luxury accents; do not make the whole city black-and-gold.
- Wet pavement and reflections are optional atmosphere, not a permanent weather condition.

### Production deliverables
1. **Hero concept image:** downtown skyline and street-level establishing shot.
2. **Landmark blockout:** Willis Tower, 875 North Michigan, Navy Pier, river, bridges, and elevated tracks positioned from a verified geographic reference.
3. **Street-level environment slice:** one playable-looking block with buildings, sidewalks, traffic, pedestrians, and L-train overhead.
4. **Transit integration note:** track path, station/stop location, train clearance, and future rideable-train requirements.
5. **QA pass:** check landmark orientation, street continuity, train-track continuity, scale, lighting, and asset compatibility.

### Agent handoff
- **Trinity:** coordinate the brief, dependencies, and acceptance checks.
- **Atlas:** produce the cinematic visual composition and camera references.
- **Blueprint:** establish block layout, building massing, and landmark placement.
- **Vibe:** define lighting, time-of-day, color transitions, and atmosphere.
- **Stitch:** review cross-asset fit and visual continuity.
- **Motion:** plan the north-to-south camera movement and moving train/traffic beats.
- **Tracks:** validate elevated rail geometry and station integration.

### Acceptance criteria
- Willis Tower and 875 North Michigan are both recognizable and not confused.
- Navy Pier and its Ferris wheel read as lakefront landmarks.
- The CTA “L” is visibly elevated and structurally plausible.
- The composition supports a north-to-south journey.
- The palette includes the established blue/white and pink/blue looks, with selective warm gold at night.
- The result feels like a lived-in Chicago district and can be decomposed into reusable game assets.

### Important implementation note
This brief records the creator-approved direction. It does **not** claim that local Termux agents have executed, that a 3D scene exists, or that the generated concept image is already a game asset. Local execution and in-engine validation remain separate production steps.
