# Hustle City — Social World, Lifestyle & Player Interaction Requirements

**Status:** APPROVED CREATOR DIRECTION — captured from the creator's explicit feature requirements. Detailed UX, content, technical implementation, production scope and balancing remain to be specified.

## 1. Product priority

Hustle City is a persistent, cinematic, realistic 3D social-world and city-life experience. It is not primarily a poker game. Poker is one activity inside a broader world where players create identities, build lifestyles, socialize, visit businesses and venues, explore, collect and trade items, attend entertainment, and interact with other players and NPCs.

Design and production should prioritize the reusable social world and player-lifestyle foundations. Poker and other games plug into that world rather than defining the whole product.

## 2. Character creation and identity

Players must be able to create distinctive characters and customize their appearance. Required customization directions include:
- Male and female character presentation and fashion catalogs.
- Multiple body builds, including slim/skinny, average, athletic/muscular and heavyset/larger builds.
- Height and body-proportion options, with the final safe/feasible range to be designed.
- Facial structure and features, skin appearance, eyebrows and other face options.
- Facial hair choices including clean-shaven, mustache, goatee and varied beard styles/lengths.
- Multiple hairstyles and haircuts.
- Clothing, shoes, jewelry, watches, glasses, hats, bags and other accessories.
- Visually distinct personal styles and identities.

The creator will provide clothing/outfit reference images for men's and women's styles in a later work session. Treat those references as visual direction, not automatically licensed or production-ready assets.

## 3. Wardrobe, outfits and changing locations

Players must be able to manage clothing as individual inventory/equipment items and change their look in the 3D world:
- Change jackets, shirts/tops, pants/bottoms, shoes and accessories individually.
- Assemble and save complete outfit combinations.
- Browse owned clothing in an apartment/house wardrobe or walk-in closet.
- Use designated wardrobe/changing facilities at other locations, including Hustle Towers and appropriate private changing rooms.
- See the character preview wearing selected items; a mirror-based 3D preview is the desired presentation direction.
- Maintain owned clothing and saved outfits as part of the player's persistent identity.

## 4. Weather-aware clothing

The world should support clothing choices appropriate to changing weather and seasons, including summer heat, rain, cold evenings and winter conditions. Examples include coats, sweaters, boots, rain jackets, layered clothing, shorts, T-shirts, dresses and sandals.

Automatic outfit recommendations may be considered, but players retain control. Weather should not arbitrarily remove clothing from inventory or force a change unless a later approved gameplay rule says otherwise.

## 5. Travel bags and packing

Players must be able to prepare for trips by selecting a travel bag or suitcase and packing clothing for a weekend or other trip:
- Select owned clothing and shoes to pack.
- Store packed items in a travel bag with a capacity that will be defined during inventory design.
- Carry or transport the bag to a vehicle and travel destination.
- Retain the packed contents while traveling.
- Access the bag and change outfits at suitable destinations, such as hotels or accommodations.

Exact bag sizes, capacity units, weight rules, travel destinations and luggage animations remain to be designed.

## 6. Food and restaurant catalog

The world must include believable restaurants and broad item-level menus, including:
- Soul food bowls and full soul food plates.
- Fried chicken, smothered pork chops, oxtails, catfish and other comfort-food plates.
- Rib-tip dinners, barbecue and hot links.
- Shrimp dinners and seafood combinations.
- Hamburgers, cheeseburgers, fries, sandwiches, wings and chicken baskets.
- Chicago-style food such as Italian beef, Chicago hot dogs, Maxwell Street Polish, Italian sausage and pizza puffs.
- Pizza, including deep-dish and tavern-style varieties, slices and whole pies.
- Tacos, burritos, quesadillas, nachos, rice and beans.
- Breakfast, sides, snacks, desserts and late-night food.

This is a required menu direction, not a finalized exhaustive menu. Item names, portions, venue assignments and game prices require catalog work and creator review. Use sourced real-world restaurant prices as reference evidence. Preserve creator-provided affordability anchors already recorded in the economy catalog.

## 7. Non-alcoholic and alcoholic beverages

The catalog must include soft drinks/pop/soda, water, juice and other non-alcoholic beverages, as well as branded and generic alcoholic beverages where permitted.

Alcohol categories and examples discussed include:
- Cognac: Hennessy, D'Ussé, Rémy Martin and Courvoisier.
- Vodka: Cîroc, Grey Goose, Tito's, Belvedere and Absolut.
- Whiskey: Jack Daniel's, Crown Royal, Jameson and Maker's Mark.
- Tequila: Patrón, Don Julio, Casamigos and others.
- Rum and gin.
- Alizé and other flavored liqueurs.
- Cocktails such as Long Island Iced Tea, margaritas, daiquiris, piña coladas, tropical cocktails and other bar drinks.
- Individual servings, shots, full bottles and nightclub bottle service.

Brand availability, licensing, exact products, sizes, pour volumes and prices remain subject to research, rights review and creator approval. Distinguish retail bottle prices, ordinary bar prices and nightclub bottle-service prices.

## 8. Cigarettes, cigars and smoking props/interactions

The world should include cigarettes, cigarette packs, cigars, premium cigars and related accessories such as lighters, cigar cutters and ashtrays. Required interaction directions include:
- Pull out and light a cigarette.
- Light and smoke a cigar.
- Smoke using character animations.
- Put a cigarette out in an ashtray.
- Tap cigar ash into an ashtray.
- Place/use ashtrays as physical venue/table objects.
- Support appropriate smoking interactions at poker tables and designated cigar venues.

Exact brands, product catalog, venue rules, visual effects and animation sets remain to be specified. Treat smoking as a simulated character/prop interaction, not as a real-world health claim.

## 9. Poker-table contextual action panel

When seated at a poker table, the player should have a small optional side-action area with contextual buttons that do not replace or obstruct the primary poker interface. Initial actions include:
- Pour yourself a drink from an owned bottle.
- Drink from the bottle.
- Light a cigar.
- Pull out and light a cigarette.
- Use the table ashtray.
- Offer another player a shot.
- Access relevant social actions.

The available actions should depend on the character's inventory, seated state, venue rules and current animation/action state. Reuse common contextual-action and animation systems across the world.

## 10. Bottles, pours and persistent physical possessions

Players must be able to buy a full bottle at a poker table or other supported venue. A bottle is a persistent inventory item that can also appear as a physical object in the world. Required directions:
- Pour a drink for oneself.
- Offer/pour a shot for another player.
- Track the remaining liquid/servings based on bottle size and pour definition.
- Pick up, set down and carry a bottle.
- Leave a poker table carrying the bottle.
- Retain possession when entering/exiting a vehicle and traveling to another destination.
- Drink from a glass or directly from the bottle where supported.

Previously discussed estimates of 15–20 shots from a bottle were illustrative only. Final serving counts must be derived from verified bottle volume and defined game pour sizes; do not hardcode an unapproved quantity.

## 11. Social gifting, inbox and NPC fulfillment

Players must be able to purchase gifts for other players, including food, drinks, shots and bottles. Gift offers are delivered through the recipient's in-game messaging/inbox system.

Required interaction rules:
- The sender selects a recipient and item, then confirms the purchase.
- The recipient must accept before the gift is fulfilled.
- The recipient may decline; accepting does not charge the recipient.
- Accepted gifts can be fulfilled through venue service and NPC delivery.
- For a shot offer, the sender's character may pour the shot, then an NPC/server can carry it to the recipient.
- The recipient receives and consumes the item through an appropriate interaction/animation.
- Support gifts sent while a recipient is offline, with delivery/claim behavior to be specified.

Refund timing, gift expiry, duplicate-charge protection, delivery availability, blocking/privacy controls and edge cases remain to be designed. Do not assume final refund or expiry rules until approved.

## 12. NPC service and delivery

NPC staff should make venues feel operational rather than decorative. Reusable service behaviors should support bartenders preparing drinks, servers carrying food and drinks, and staff delivering accepted gifts to player-selected recipients or tables. A fallback is needed when no server is available so an accepted purchase does not become permanently stuck. Detailed pathfinding, service queues, staffing density and fallback behavior remain technical design work.

## 13. Cigar bars, lounges and private social rooms

Required venue concepts include:
- Cigar bars.
- Cigar lounges.
- Private cigar lounges.
- Executive/boss-style cigar rooms.
- VIP/private social rooms.

These venues support socializing, conversations, drinks, bottle service, cigars, cigarettes where permitted, card collecting/trading and player interaction. They are social destinations in their own right, not merely extensions of poker.

## 14. Character simulation and atmosphere

The desired experience includes expressive 3D character actions and environmental feedback: drinking, pouring, lighting, smoking, social gestures, NPC service, ambient smoke where appropriate, and graduated visual/animation presentation of a character becoming relaxed or bubbly. Any intoxication simulation must be designed carefully, remain nonessential to participation and confer no advantage in poker or other competitive play. Exact effects, limits and gameplay implications remain unresolved.

## 15. Shared technical foundations and build strategy

Prefer reusable systems rather than implementing each venue or item as a one-off:
- Character appearance/body customization and outfit/equipment system.
- Persistent inventory and physical-item representation.
- Wardrobe, outfit presets and travel-bag storage.
- Contextual action/menu framework.
- Reusable animation and interaction framework.
- Multiplayer state replication for visible actions and possessions.
- Social gifting, inbox and transaction ledger.
- NPC service, delivery and navigation framework.
- Weather/season data that can inform outfit recommendations.
- Venue capability/rule configuration.

Implement incrementally. A shared animation may be reused across many compatible drink items, with item-specific props and presentation. However, body-shape compatibility, clothing fit, multiplayer object persistence, synchronized animation and NPC navigation require engineering and testing; do not describe them as effortless or already implemented.

## 16. Economy and real-world price research

Use real-world restaurant, retail bottle, bar and nightclub price research as evidence for the game economy. Keep those reference types separate. Game prices, daily login rewards, gameplay earnings and membership currency allocations must be modeled together so free players can participate meaningfully while paid memberships provide additional value without making core social participation pay-to-win. Do not finalize reward quantities before major spending catalogs and sinks are modeled.

Food and drinks use Hustle Money, not Poker Chips. Keep Hustle Money, Diamonds, Poker Chips and Wings as separate wallets.

## 17. Scope and status discipline

This document records creator-required product directions. It does not mean the features are implemented, production-ready, fully specified or included in the first playable release. Continue to record:
- **Required direction:** creator has directed that the experience/feature must exist.
- **To be designed:** interaction details, data, capacities, rules, art scope, edge cases or balancing are still open.
- **Implementation status:** not started / specified / in development / tested / shipped, based only on verified project evidence.

The creator is the final design authority. Assistant and agent recommendations may improve or challenge implementation plans but cannot silently override creator requirements or turn unresolved details into canon.
