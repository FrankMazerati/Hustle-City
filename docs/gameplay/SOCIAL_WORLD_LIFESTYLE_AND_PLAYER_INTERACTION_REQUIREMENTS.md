# Hustle City — Social World, Lifestyle & Player Interaction Requirements

**Status:** APPROVED CREATOR DIRECTION — captured from the creator's explicit feature requirements. Detailed UX, content, technical implementation, production scope and balancing remain to be specified.

## 1. Product priority

Hustle City is a persistent, cinematic, realistic 3D social-world and city-life experience. It is not primarily a poker game. Poker is one activity inside a broader world where players create identities, build lifestyles, socialize, visit businesses and venues, explore, collect and trade items, attend entertainment, and interact with other players and NPCs.

Design and production should prioritize the reusable social world and player-lifestyle foundations. Poker and other games plug into that world rather than defining the whole product.

## 2. Character creation and identity

Players must be able to create distinctive characters and customize their appearance. Required customization directions include:
- Male and female character presentation and fashion catalogs.
- Multiple body builds, including slim/skinny, average, athletic/muscular and heavyset/larger builds.
- Height and body-proportion options, with the final safe/feasible range to be designed.
- Facial structure and features, skin appearance, eyebrows and other face options.
- Facial hair choices including clean-shaven, mustache, goatee and varied beard styles/lengths.
- Multiple hairstyles and haircuts.
- Clothing, shoes, jewelry, watches, glasses, hats, bags and other accessories.
- Visually distinct personal styles and identities.

The creator will provide clothing/outfit reference images for men's and women's styles in a later work session. Treat those references as visual direction, not automatically licensed or production-ready assets.

## 3. Wardrobe, outfits and changing locations

Players must be able to manage clothing as individual inventory/equipment items and change their look in the 3D world:
- Change jackets, shirts/tops, pants/bottoms, shoes and accessories individually.
- Assemble and save complete outfit combinations.
- Browse owned clothing in an apartment/house wardrobe or walk-in closet.
- Use designated wardrobe/changing facilities at other locations, including Hustle Towers and appropriate private changing rooms.
- See the character preview wearing selected items; a mirror-based 3D preview is the desired presentation direction.
- Maintain owned clothing and saved outfits as part of the player's persistent identity.

## 4. Weather-aware clothing

The world should support clothing choices appropriate to changing weather and seasons, including summer heat, rain, cold evenings and winter conditions. Examples include coats, sweaters, boots, rain jackets, layered clothing, shorts, T-shirts, dresses and sandals.

Automatic outfit recommendations may be considered, but players retain control. Weather should not arbitrarily remove clothing from inventory or force a change unless a later approved gameplay rule says otherwise.

## 5. Travel bags and packing

Players must be able to prepare for trips by selecting a travel bag or suitcase and packing clothing for a weekend or other trip:
- Select owned clothing and shoes to pack.
- Store packed items in a travel bag with a capacity that will be defined during inventory design.
- Carry or transport the bag to a vehicle and travel destination.
- Retain the packed contents while traveling.
- Access the bag and change outfits at suitable destinations, such as hotels or accommodations.

Exact bag sizes, capacity units, weight rules, travel destinations and luggage animations remain to be designed.

## 6. Food and restaurant catalog

The world must include believable restaurants and broad item-level menus, including:
- Soul food bowls and full soul food plates.
- Fried chicken, smothered pork chops, oxtails, catfish and other comfort-food plates.
- Rib-tip dinners, barbecue and hot links.
- Shrimp dinners and seafood combinations.
- Hamburgers, cheeseburgers, fries, sandwiches, wings and chicken baskets.
- Chicago-style food such as Italian beef, Chicago hot dogs, Maxwell Street Polish, Italian sausage and pizza puffs.
- Pizza, including deep-dish and tavern-style varieties, slices and whole pies.
- Tacos, burritos, quesadillas, nachos, rice and beans.
- Breakfast, sides, snacks, desserts and late-night food.

This is a required menu direction, not a finalized exhaustive menu. Item names, portions, venue assignments and game prices require catalog work and creator review. Use sourced real-world restaurant prices as reference evidence. Preserve creator-provided affordability anchors already recorded in the economy catalog.

## 7. Non-alcoholic and alcoholic beverages

The catalog must include soft drinks/pop/soda, water, juice and other non-alcoholic beverages, as well as branded and generic alcoholic beverages where permitted.

Alcohol categories and examples discussed include:
- Cognac: Hennessy, D'Ussé, Rémy Martin and Courvoisier.
- Vodka: Cîroc, Grey Goose, Tito's, Belvedere and Absolut.
- Whiskey: Jack Daniel's, Crown Royal, Jameson and Maker's Mark.
- Tequila: Patrón, Don Julio, Casamigos and others.
- Rum and gin.
- Alizé and other flavored liqueurs.
- Cocktails such as Long Island Iced Tea, margaritas, daiquiris, piña coladas, tropical cocktails and other bar drinks.
- Individual servings, shots, full bottles and nightclub bottle service.

Brand availability, licensing, exact products, sizes, pour volumes and prices remain subject to research, rights review and creator approval. Distinguish retail bottle prices, ordinary bar prices and nightclub bottle-service prices.

## 8. Cigarettes, cigars and smoking props/interactions

The world should include cigarettes, cigarette packs, cigars, premium cigars and related accessories such as lighters, cigar cutters and ashtrays. Required interaction directions include:
- Pull out and light a cigarette.
- Light and smoke a cigar.
- Smoke using character animations.
- Put a cigarette out in an ashtray.
- Tap cigar ash into an ashtray.
- Place/use ashtrays as physical venue/table objects.
- Support appropriate smoking interactions at poker tables and designated cigar venues.

Exact brands, product catalog, venue rules, visual effects and animation sets remain to be specified. Treat smoking as a simulated character/prop interaction, not as a real-world health claim.

## 9. Poker-table contextual action panel

When seated at a poker table, the player should have a small optional side-action area with contextual buttons that do not replace or obstruct the primary poker interface. Initial actions include:
- Pour yourself a drink from an owned bottle.
- Drink from the bottle.
- Light a cigar.
- Pull out and light a cigarette.
- Use the table ashtray.
- Offer another player a shot.
- Access relevant social actions.

The available actions should depend on the character's inventory, seated state, venue rules and current animation/action state. Reuse common contextual-action and animation systems across the world.

## 10. Bottles, pours and persistent physical possessions

Players must be able to buy a full bottle at a poker table or other supported venue. A bottle is a persistent inventory item that can also appear as a physical object in the world. Required directions:
- Pour a drink for oneself.
- Offer/pour a shot for another player.
- Track the remaining liquid/servings based on bottle size and pour definition.
- Pick up, set down and carry a bottle.
- Leave a poker table carrying the bottle.
- Retain possession when entering/exiting a vehicle and traveling to another destination.
- Drink from a glass or directly from the bottle where supported.

Previously discussed estimates of 15–20 shots from a bottle were illustrative only. Final serving counts must be derived from verified bottle volume and defined game pour sizes; do not hardcode an unapproved quantity.

## 11. Social gifting, inbox and NPC fulfillment

Players must be able to purchase gifts for other players, including food, drinks, shots and bottles. Gift offers are delivered through the recipient's in-game messaging/inbox system.

Required interaction rules:
- The sender selects a recipient and item, then confirms the purchase.
- The recipient must accept before the gift is fulfilled.
- The recipient may decline; accepting does not charge the recipient.
- Accepted gifts can be fulfilled through venue service and NPC delivery.
- For a shot offer, the sender's character may pour the shot, then an NPC/server can carry it to the recipient.
- The recipient receives and consumes the item through an appropriate interaction/animation.
- Support gifts sent while a recipient is offline, with delivery/claim behavior to be specified.

Refund timing, gift expiry, duplicate-charge protection, delivery availability, blocking/privacy controls and edge cases remain to be designed. Do not assume final refund or expiry rules until approved.

## 12. NPC service and delivery

NPC staff should make venues feel operational rather than decorative. Reusable service behaviors should support bartenders preparing drinks, servers carrying food and drinks, and staff delivering accepted gifts to player-selected recipients or tables. A fallback is needed when no server is available so an accepted purchase does not become permanently stuck. Detailed pathfinding, service queues, staffing density and fallback behavior remain technical design work.

## 13. Cigar bars, lounges and private social rooms

Required venue concepts include:
- Cigar bars.
- Cigar lounges.
- Private cigar lounges.
- Executive/boss-style cigar rooms.
- VIP/private social rooms.

These venues support socializing, conversations, drinks, bottle service, cigars, cigarettes where permitted, card collecting/trading and player interaction. They are social destinations in their own right, not merely extensions of poker.

## 14. Character simulation and atmosphere

The desired experience includes expressive 3D character actions and environmental feedback: drinking, pouring, lighting, smoking, social gestures, NPC service, ambient smoke where appropriate, and graduated visual/animation presentation of a character becoming relaxed or bubbly. Any intoxication simulation must be designed carefully, remain nonessential to participation and confer no advantage in poker or other competitive play. Exact effects, limits and gameplay implications remain unresolved.

## 15. Shared technical foundations and build strategy

Prefer reusable systems rather than implementing each venue or item as a one-off:
- Character appearance/body customization and outfit/equipment system.
- Persistent inventory and physical-item representation.
- Wardrobe, outfit presets and travel-bag storage.
- Contextual action/menu framework.
- Reusable animation and interaction framework.
- Multiplayer state replication for visible actions and possessions.
- Social gifting, inbox and transaction ledger.
- NPC service, delivery and navigation framework.
- Weather/season data that can inform outfit recommendations.
- Venue capability/rule configuration.

Implement incrementally. A shared animation may be reused across many compatible drink items, with item-specific props and presentation. However, body-shape compatibility, clothing fit, multiplayer object persistence, synchronized animation and NPC navigation require engineering and testing; do not describe them as effortless or already implemented.

## 16. Economy and real-world price research

Use real-world restaurant, retail bottle, bar and nightclub price research as evidence for the game economy. Keep those reference types separate. Game prices, daily login rewards, gameplay earnings and membership currency allocations must be modeled together so free players can participate meaningfully while paid memberships provide additional value without making core social participation pay-to-win. Do not finalize reward quantities before major spending catalogs and sinks are modeled.

Food and drinks use Hustle Money, not Poker Chips. Keep Hustle Money, Diamonds, Poker Chips and Wings as separate wallets.

## 17. Scope and status discipline

This document records creator-required product directions. It does not mean the features are implemented, production-ready, fully specified or included in the first playable release. Continue to record:
- **Required direction:** creator has directed that the experience/feature must exist.
- **To be designed:** interaction details, data, capacities, rules, art scope, edge cases or balancing are still open.
- **Implementation status:** not started / specified / in development / tested / shipped, based only on verified project evidence.

The creator is the final design authority. Assistant and agent recommendations may improve or challenge implementation plans but cannot silently override creator requirements or turn unresolved details into canon.

## 18. Hotel hospitality, valet and Hustle Towers services

Hotels and appropriate upscale residential/commercial towers must feel staffed and operational. Required service roles and interactions include:
- Doormen who greet arriving players and assist at entrances.
- A circular driveway / porte-cochère drop-off experience where a player can pull up, exit the vehicle and hand it over to a valet.
- Valet/parking attendants who take the player's vehicle to parking and can retrieve/return it when requested.
- A concierge desk where players can approach staff, ask questions and request assistance.
- Front-desk staff for check-in and hotel services.
- Bellhops/luggage attendants who can assist with travel bags.
- Elevator attendants who greet guests, assist with elevator use and direct players to floors or facilities.
- Staff who can give directions to rooms, lounges, restaurants, meeting spaces and other destinations.
- Comparable hospitality interactions at Hustle Towers where appropriate.

The desired vehicle flow is visible and physical: arrive at the circular driveway, exit, hand off the vehicle, see the valet take it away, then request and receive it when leaving. Vehicle ownership and persistence must remain intact throughout.

Exact hotel systems, parking layouts, valet routing, fees, staff schedules, elevator controls and service availability remain to be designed.

## 19. Conversational, proactive and context-aware NPCs

NPCs must be active participants in the social world, not merely static scenery or silent counter staff. Their purpose includes helping players understand and participate in the world through ongoing, two-way communication.

Required direction:
- NPCs can proactively greet or approach players when contextually appropriate.
- NPCs can ask players questions and allow the player to respond.
- NPCs can react to player responses with contextually appropriate follow-up dialogue.
- NPCs can answer questions about nearby businesses, venues, services, activities, events, directions and relevant game systems.
- NPCs can explain how to use local services and what a player can do in the current location.
- NPC conversations should reflect the venue, neighborhood, role, current activity and recognizable Hustle City voice/personality.
- Staff NPCs should have role-appropriate dialogue: concierge, doorman, valet, elevator attendant, front desk, bellhop, host, waiter, bartender, shopkeeper, security and other city workers.
- Neighborhood residents and other city personalities should contribute to the feeling of ongoing local conversation and city communication.
- Players should be able to choose or enter responses; NPC dialogue should not be a one-way monologue.
- NPC dialogue must use authoritative game/world state for factual claims about player inventory, transactions, events, schedules, locations and services. NPCs must not invent game events, claim actions succeeded when they did not, or hallucinate player-specific information.
- NPC communication should help players discover activities and feel welcomed and informed without becoming constant, intrusive or blocking gameplay.

This is a core living-world requirement. The exact dialogue architecture, memory/context boundaries, interaction UI, voice/text presentation, language/localization, concurrency and runtime model strategy remain technical design work.

## 20. Large multi-table poker rooms and ambient venue population

Poker rooms must support the feeling of a substantial, active entertainment venue rather than a single isolated table or small empty room. Required world-design direction:
- A large room containing multiple poker tables operating within the same venue.
- From a player's table, the room should reveal other tables, player groups, NPCs and venue activity across the space.
- Multiple player and NPC groups can occupy different tables and areas simultaneously.
- NPC staff circulate through the room on purposeful service routes, approaching different tables and players.
- Staff navigation should avoid repeatedly obstructing seated players, active gameplay, camera views and table access.
- The room should include supporting hospitality areas and staff activity appropriate to the venue.
- Ambient conversation, service dialogue, movement and background activity should make the room feel populated and socially active.

Exact room dimensions, table count, occupancy limits, NPC population density, performance budgets and streaming/LOD strategy remain to be designed and tested.

## 21. Roaming poker-room service NPCs and spoken ordering

Poker-room service must be proactive as well as menu-driven. NPC servers should circulate among tables and approach players who may want service rather than requiring every player to open a menu first.

Required interaction direction:
- NPC servers can approach an individual player or table and audibly offer service, with lines such as asking whether the player wants a drink or something to eat.
- Players can answer using contextual dialogue/interaction choices, voice or text input as supported by the eventual interface.
- The NPC can present available food and drink choices, take the order and communicate the order back.
- The NPC then travels to the appropriate bar, kitchen, service station or inventory source, obtains the ordered item and returns to deliver it.
- The purchase is charged to the correct player only after the order is confirmed under the finalized transaction flow.
- Delivery updates the player's item state and is represented by visible character/object animations.
- Servers should be aware of relevant service context, such as whether a player already has a drink or food, whether the player is busy/in an unavailable state, and whether the venue can fulfill the requested item.
- Players can decline or dismiss an offer; NPCs should respect the response and avoid pestering.
- Service must coexist with poker gameplay and the room's other activities.

The desired visual direction includes predominantly adult female hospitality staff, with attractive, glamorous, confident presentation and venue-appropriate fashionable or revealing uniforms, including lingerie-inspired styling where suitable to the venue's rating, platform rules and setting. Staff should be treated as fully realized characters with professional service roles, not static decorative bodies. Final wardrobe designs and visual references remain to be supplied/reviewed by the creator.

NPC service dialogue should be audible and context-aware. Voice lines, dialogue selection, speech/audio settings, localization, NPC behavior state, pathfinding, service queues, transaction handling and performance requirements remain implementation design work.

## 22. Flirting, playful banter and social chemistry

The social world must support playful, charming, flirtatious interactions between adult characters. NPCs and players should be able to exchange natural, context-sensitive banter that adds warmth, humor and personality to venues and everyday city life.

Required interaction direction:
- Adult female NPCs may flirt playfully with adult male players, and adult male NPCs may flirt playfully with adult female players.
- Players can respond positively, flirt back, joke, change the subject or politely decline.
- NPCs can initiate light, situational banter where appropriate, including hospitality interactions while greeting guests, taking orders or serving tables.
- Characters can smile, laugh, make eye contact, use expressive facial reactions and conversational body language.
- Dialogue can include playful teasing, compliments and remarks about a player's outfit, hairstyle or accessories, without making every interaction romantic or repetitive.
- Appropriate friendly gestures can include a brief shoulder touch or similar light contact, but physical contact must be context-sensitive and governed by interaction/consent settings. No forced intimate contact or harassment.
- NPCs should recognize a player's response and respect disinterest, refusal, blocking or social boundaries. Repeated unwanted advances must not occur.
- Flirting should be optional social flavor, not a requirement for service, progression, purchases or access to venues.
- Keep romantic/flirtatious interactions between adult characters; do not sexualize minors or ambiguous-age characters.

Dialogue personality, relationship/memory depth, player-to-player consent controls, animation sets, voice performance and localization remain to be designed.

## 23. Contextual quick replies and free-text NPC conversation

NPC conversations must give players fast, context-relevant response choices while also allowing custom typed replies. Players should not have to type every routine response, but should not be limited to canned dialogue.

Required behavior:
- When an NPC asks a question or offers a service, present a small set of quick-response choices tailored to that exact prompt, NPC role, venue, player state and available services.
- For a drink offer, quick replies can include relevant available drink categories/items, asking to see the drink selection, and declining. The actual choices must reflect what that NPC and venue can provide.
- For food offers, show relevant food options or a way to view the menu, plus decline/other appropriate responses.
- For cigar attendants, choices can include available cigar options, asking to see the selection, asking a question, or declining another cigar. Only offer items/services actually available in that venue and NPC role.
- Apply the same contextual approach to valet, concierge, front desk, bellhop, elevator attendant, poker-room server, bartender and other NPC interactions. For example, valet choices should relate to retrieving or parking a vehicle, not food or drinks.
- Include a custom text-entry option so the player can type a specific question, request, joke, compliment, flirtatious response or other natural-language reply.
- NPC responses to custom text must account for the conversation so far, NPC role/personality, venue context, player intent and authoritative game state. The NPC must not claim to perform an unavailable action or invent stock, prices, events, directions or player information.
- Quick replies and free-text input must coexist in the same conversation experience. Players can use quick replies for speed and switch to custom text whenever they want.
- Choices should be concise, readable and usable on mobile/touch interfaces; avoid covering essential gameplay or poker controls.
- If a player declines, the NPC should acknowledge it naturally and stop pressing the offer. Repeated prompts must respect the player's response and current state.
- When a choice selects a purchasable item or service, clearly communicate the selection and applicable price before charging where needed; confirm the order/action, then fulfill it through the appropriate service workflow. Do not charge for a declined or unconfirmed purchase.
- The system must support conversation continuity: NPCs should respond to the player's latest reply rather than treating each message as an unrelated interaction.

Interaction presentation, exact number/layout of quick replies, text-entry UI, moderation/safety, speech-to-text/voice options, localization, dialogue orchestration and runtime model strategy remain to be designed.


## 24. Universal role-based NPC interaction across the city

The contextual quick-reply and free-text conversation system is a shared, world-wide interaction foundation. It must work wherever players interact with an NPC, not only in restaurants, bars or poker rooms.

Required behavior:
- Apply role-aware conversations to valet attendants, hotel front-desk staff, concierges, doormen, bellhops/luggage attendants, elevator attendants, hosts, servers, bartenders, cigar attendants, shopkeepers, security staff, transportation workers and other appropriate NPC roles.
- Quick replies must match the NPC's actual responsibilities and the player's current situation. A valet can offer to park or retrieve a vehicle; a front-desk clerk can help book or check into an available room; a concierge can provide directions or information; a bellhop can help with luggage; an elevator attendant can help select a floor or destination.
- Support complete service journeys where appropriate: request, clarify details, show valid options, confirm the player's selection, perform the action, and report the verified result. Examples include vehicle drop-off and retrieval, room search/booking/check-in, directions and destination guidance, luggage assistance, elevator/facility access and other venue services.
- For reservations, purchases or other consequential actions, show the relevant available options and terms (including price or fees when applicable) and obtain the required player confirmation before committing. Do not imply a room, vehicle, service or destination is secured until the authoritative game system confirms it.
- Provide a custom text-entry route alongside quick replies so players can ask unusual questions, give specific instructions, make special requests or simply converse naturally. NPCs should understand and respond to the intent when supported; otherwise they should clarify or explain the available alternatives rather than pretend to comply.
- Conversations should remain continuous across follow-up questions and actions, and should reflect NPC role, venue, player context, available services, current world state and prior turns in the interaction.
- NPCs must use authoritative game/service systems for availability, prices, booking status, vehicle ownership/location, route/destination data and action outcomes. Never fabricate directions, room availability, booking confirmations, fees, vehicle retrieval, inventory or completed actions.
- Keep interactions touch-friendly and concise. Quick replies should reduce friction without preventing free-form conversation or interrupting core gameplay.
- The same framework should be extensible to future NPC roles and services through role/service configuration rather than one-off conversation implementations.

Exact interaction layouts, booking and reservation rules, payment/fee policies, service APIs, navigation data, dialogue orchestration, error recovery and implementation phasing remain to be designed.

## 25. Distinct NPC identity, personality, voice and style

NPCs must be individually recognizable characters with their own identity and presence. They should not feel like interchangeable copies differentiated only by job title.

Required creator direction:
- Give NPCs distinct personalities, conversational attitudes, humor, confidence, warmth, temperament and social energy appropriate to their role and background.
- Give NPCs distinctive voices, including varied vocal tone, cadence, delivery and speaking style. Voice casting/creation must support recognizable character identity and avoid every NPC sounding alike.
- Give NPCs distinct visual looks and personal styles: clothing, grooming, hair, accessories, posture, movement and overall presentation.
- Reflect individual swagger, confidence and mannerisms through dialogue, facial expression, gesture, walk cycles and idle behavior.
- Keep character design coherent with venue, occupation, neighborhood, age-appropriate presentation and local setting while allowing memorable individual variation.
- NPC personality and style should carry into quick replies, free-text responses, flirting, service interactions and ambient conversations; the same character should remain recognizable across encounters.
- NPCs should not all use the same catchphrases, flirt, approach players or behave with the same level of energy. Their social approach should reflect their individual personality and context.
- Distinct identity must not undermine service accuracy, player boundaries or the authoritative game-state rules governing bookings, purchases, inventory and other actions.

Exact character roster, voice approach, dialogue performance, visual archetypes, animation sets, memory/continuity depth and production budgets remain to be designed.


## 26. Social participation, player networking and optional community sharing

Hustle City should encourage players to build relationships and participate in the community as part of the living social world.

Creator direction:
- Support player-to-player communication and friend discovery/adding as core social features.
- Support optional social posts or sharing of in-game moments/progress.
- Social participation may become an additional gameplay earning or reward path, alongside poker and other activities, but exact qualifying actions, reward types, amounts, cadence and caps remain unresolved.
- Do not require players to publish to external social media, expose personal information or post publicly to access core gameplay or progression.
- Build protections against spam, fake accounts, repeated low-effort posts, referral abuse, collusion and engagement farming. Rewards must be idempotent, rate-limited and validated by authoritative server-side rules.
- Provide appropriate privacy, blocking, reporting and audience controls for player communications and shared content.

Social feature UI, eligible activities, reward budget, moderation and privacy implementation remain to be designed.

## 27. Neighborhood parcel identity and home ownership experience

Neighborhood homes should be represented as individually identifiable, persistent properties rather than anonymous interchangeable house models.

Creator direction:
- Homes may be listed for sale when vacant/unoccupied and available for purchase.
- Every home/parcel needs a stable unique ID linked to its street address, city block and neighborhood.
- The property system must display authoritative availability/occupancy and ownership status, preventing a player from purchasing a home that is occupied, reserved or already owned.
- Players may be able to own multiple homes on one block and potentially acquire an entire block. Five homes or a whole block were given as examples; exact ownership caps and acquisition rules are not finalized.
- Preserve the distinction between individual property ownership, owning several homes, whole-block ownership and founding/claiming a neighborhood. A home or block purchase does not automatically grant neighborhood-founder status.
- The accepted world layout is 7×7 large boxes (49 total), with six central districts and 43 neighborhood boxes. Each neighborhood box has a 5×5 grid of city blocks (25 per neighborhood box). This establishes the city-block structure, not the number of houses on a block.

Proposed planning range only: approximately 5–10 houses on a city block. The final count, parcel sizes, prices, purchase currency, property types, ownership caps, rental/resale/transfer rules, taxes/maintenance and whole-block purchase behavior remain open for creator decision.

Implementation should use authoritative parcel records, unique IDs, transaction history, concurrency-safe purchase processing, explicit purchase confirmation and clear owner/occupancy states.


## 28. Neighborhood economic identity, localized pricing and NPC populations

Hustle City neighborhoods must have distinct economic identities. The city should not feel as though every neighborhood is the same environment with a different sign.

Creator direction:
- Establish a range of neighborhood identities, including affluent/upscale areas, middle-income areas, working-class areas and lower-income/hood areas.
- Give neighborhoods distinct names and recognizable identities. Final neighborhood names and the complete tier map remain to be determined.
- Prices for comparable goods and services may differ by neighborhood. Local restaurants, bars, shops, salons, transportation/service providers and other businesses should have pricing appropriate to their neighborhood positioning, clientele, operating costs and service level.
- Do not assume a single universal citywide price for every item or service. The economy must support neighborhood-specific price data and clearly defined exceptions for chain-wide or standardized prices.
- Neighborhood pricing should be authored and balanced deliberately; do not generate arbitrary price differences or change existing catalog prices during this requirements pass.
- NPC populations should reflect the neighborhood's social and economic character through believable variation in clothing, grooming, vehicles, occupations, conversation, behavior, leisure activities and use of local businesses and public spaces.
- Upscale neighborhoods should feel upscale through the complete environment and population, not merely expensive storefronts. Hood and working-class neighborhoods should feel authentic and lived-in through local businesses, residents, street activity and distinct styles, without making every resident a stereotype or reducing a community to criminality.
- NPCs remain individually distinct, with varied personalities, backgrounds, voices and appearances; neighborhood identity informs but does not dictate every character.
- Neighborhood identity must connect with the established contrast between upscale Hustle District and gritty Trapper District while allowing variation across the wider residential map.

To be designed: canonical neighborhood names and economic classifications; item/service price tables and localized modifiers; which businesses use local versus chain pricing; economic simulation inputs; NPC archetype distribution and schedules; visual/audio style guides; and balancing/QA procedures. No exact price multipliers or neighborhood-specific prices are approved by this direction alone.

## 29. Modest daily rewards and petty-cash earning opportunities

Free players should have small, dependable ways to earn enough Hustle Money to participate in ordinary city life, while larger purchases, luxury ownership and premium activities retain meaningful progression and premium value.

Creator direction:
- Add a daily login reward as a candidate baseline earning route for all eligible players, including free players.
- Add several modest petty-cash earning opportunities beyond login rewards, integrated naturally into city life. The exact activities, qualification rules, frequency, payouts and limits remain to be designed.
- The intended scale is everyday spending money: enough to buy ordinary drinks, food and occasional low-cost lifestyle items, not enough to trivialize major purchases or premium activities.
- Preserve optional membership/kit value and premium activity incentives without making paid access mandatory for core city participation.
- Daily rewards and petty-cash routes must not award collectible cards unless the creator separately approves a card-acquisition rule. Keep reward wallets and card progression distinct.
- Rewards must be credited through authoritative, idempotent server-side transactions, with clear claim status and protections against duplicate claims, clock manipulation, multi-account abuse, bots and farming exploits.
- Social participation can be considered as one optional petty-cash/reward route, but must not require external public posting or reward spam, fake engagement or disclosure of personal information.

**Proposed values only — not approved final tuning:** The creator floated approximately $500 Hustle Money per day as a modest baseline, potentially enough for ordinary food, drinks and occasional cigars; a possible 5,000–10,000 Poker Chip daily grant; and a seven-day progression in which the cash reward could rise by about $100. A much larger initial Poker Chip amount (100,000) was also mentioned as an example and then reconsidered. None of these quantities or schedules is locked. The seven-day design could be a progressive reward, but its exact cadence, streak requirements, reset and catch-up rules are unresolved.

Balancing must account for the established Poker Chip-to-Hustle Money conversion, all currency sources and sinks, ordinary item prices, membership/kit contents, play frequency, and premium activity costs. The daily grant must not create an easy route to luxury purchases or undermine the reason to pursue kits and premium content.


## 30. Everyday living costs, transportation, convenience retail and personal care

Hustle City should support everyday city-life routines in which players pay for transportation, maintain vehicles, buy food and drinks, and purchase/use personal-care products. These systems create believable recurring expenses and give modest Hustle Money earning opportunities a practical purpose.

Creator direction:
- **Paid rideshare:** Players may request rides through fictional in-world rideshare services inspired by real services such as Uber and Lyft. Rides are paid services, not a free travel shortcut. Fares should be grounded in a believable real-world-style pricing model and may account for trip distance, duration, service class and applicable local pricing. Exact fare formula, minimum fare, surge rules, tips and service tiers remain to be designed.
- **Vehicle fuel:** Player-owned fuel-powered vehicles should require fuel. Players can drive into gas stations, interact with pumps, choose a quantity or fill level, pay Hustle Money and refuel. Fuel use/cost should be coherent with vehicle type, fuel capacity, consumption and trip distance. Electric vehicles, if supported, need a corresponding charging system rather than gasoline.
- **Gas-station convenience retail:** Gas stations should include convenience-store interiors or connected retail spaces where players can browse and buy available food, drinks, snacks and personal/lifestyle goods.
- **Convenience stores:** Provide walk-in neighborhood convenience stores with stocked shelves, checkout and purchases such as chips, candy, packaged snacks, bottled/canned drinks, soda/pop, water and other approved catalog items. Product availability and price can reflect neighborhood identity and local pricing rules.

## Functional businesses, player jobs and phased city activation

**Approved creator direction — functional world rule:** Hustle City should be built around functional businesses and purposeful locations, not rows of decorative storefronts. A building presented as a business should have a real gameplay purpose: a player-facing service, purchasable goods, an activity, employment/earning opportunity, social use, or another defined interaction. The goal is that commercial buildings are actual businesses rather than empty background facades.

**NPC-operated business ecosystem:**
- Populate businesses with role-appropriate NPC owners, employees, service workers, customers and ambient visitors.
- NPCs should operate the business, provide products/services, interact conversationally with players and support the location's activities.
- Reuse common business, inventory, checkout, service, staffing, dialogue, animation and transaction systems across different business types while preserving each venue's unique identity.
- Examples include barbershops, beauty/braiding salons, nail salons, car washes, gas stations, convenience stores, clothing and retail stores, restaurants, shopping malls, lounges, casinos, concert venues and nightlife destinations.
- Do not claim every structure must be a retail business: residential, civic, transportation, hospitality and other non-commercial buildings must likewise have a coherent world purpose and appropriate interactions if included as player-facing locations. Avoid purposeless buildings presented as meaningful destinations.

**Player employment and side hustles:**
- Give players ways to take jobs, work shifts or perform side hustles that earn in-game money and provide something purposeful to do in the city.
- Candidate job categories include working at restaurants/retail, service and hospitality roles, delivery and errands, car washing/detailing, automotive support, event/concert staffing, and other neighborhood services.
- Jobs and side hustles should connect to the functional business network: businesses have staffing/service needs, players can perform suitable work, and completed work can produce an authoritative in-game payment.
- NPC-run businesses remain active and useful even when no player employee is online; player employment should complement, not be required for, a business to operate.
- Job selection, qualifications, schedules, shift length, task loops, earnings, progression, employer ownership, player-run businesses and exact payouts remain to be designed. Candidate examples are not a locked job catalog or payout schedule.

**Service and activity simulations:**
- Support visible, character-driven service experiences where appropriate. Examples include entering a barbershop, selecting a haircut or beard service, sitting in the barber chair, seeing a barber NPC perform the service animation, and seeing the player's hairstyle/appearance update after completion and payment.
- Beauty and braiding salons can offer appropriate hairstyle and braiding services; nail salons can offer nail services and appearance customization. Exact style catalogs, prices, service duration and animation coverage remain to be designed.
- Car washes should provide an actual vehicle-cleaning service and visible vehicle presentation change, not just a menu-only purchase.
- Retail businesses should support entering, browsing/selecting products, checkout/payment and receiving purchased items where applicable.
- Chess is an approved activity direction: players should be able to play chess with NPCs and, where supported, other players. Barbershops and other neighborhood gathering spots may host chess tables. Chess rules, clocks, matchmaking, rewards and competitive ranking remain to be designed.
- Street dice, card games, casino games and chess should help create recognizable neighborhood social activity, subject to the separate gambling review requirements for wagering games.

**Phased playable-world rollout:**
- Build and release the city in phases rather than requiring every planned building, neighborhood and entertainment destination to be fully playable before the first release.
- The initial release should contain a deliberately selected, coherent playable area with genuinely functioning businesses, NPC services, activities, earning opportunities and spending loops.
- Other planned buildings and areas may remain visible as future destinations and be clearly designated "Coming Soon" or equivalent until their gameplay is implemented and activated.
- As the player population and production capacity grow, additional businesses, blocks, neighborhoods, shopping areas, malls, concerts, nightlife and other destinations can be activated in planned waves.
- A location must not be presented as open or functional until its services and player-facing interactions are actually available. Coming-soon presentation should be clear and should not mislead players into expecting unavailable functionality.
- Prefer a world/content configuration that can change a location's activation state without rebuilding the entire city. Plan for states such as planned, visible-coming-soon, playable/open, temporarily unavailable and retired/renovated; exact state model and player messaging remain technical design decisions.
- Rollout sequencing should be driven by a balanced playable loop and verified readiness, not simply by the number of buildings rendered. Each activated business should have defined purpose, staffing/NPC behavior, interaction, products/services or activities, payment/earning rules, and appropriate feed/event integration.

**Implementation and scope discipline:** This direction establishes the functional-business principle, player job/side-hustle capability, barber/beauty/nail/car-wash/retail service simulations, chess activity and phased activation model. It does not mean every business type is implemented in the first release. Initial playable-area boundaries, first-wave business list, staffing density, job catalog, earnings, business ownership, service catalogs, animation coverage, activation schedule and technical architecture remain to be designed and creator-approved.

## Creator direction — player condition, food routines and social relationships
### Food, health and energy — required gameplay direction; detailed model remains open

- Players should be able to purchase food and drinks from restaurants, convenience stores/gas stations and other appropriate businesses, and eat at suitable locations including their own houses.
- Food should have a gameplay purpose: helping maintain or restore the player's condition so the character can continue participating in city life. At poker tables, the previously approved direction is that players buy food and drinks with **Hustle Money**, not Poker Chips, and different menu items have different prices and restoration effects.
- Character activity should matter. Extended poker sessions, substantial walking, traveling between destinations and other sustained activities may deplete energy/condition over time, creating a reason to take breaks, eat, drink or rest.
- The creator is considering a realistic meal routine (for example, two or three meals across a day), but does **not** want the system to become excessive or turn ordinary play into constant maintenance. Treat meal frequency, hunger/energy decay and any daily schedule as unresolved design parameters, not fixed rules.
- Eating at home should be supported as an alternative to buying every meal outside. Restaurant and grocery/convenience purchases should be useful choices, not mandatory chores.
- Keep the distinction between **health**, **energy/stamina**, **hunger/nutrition** and any other condition meters unresolved until the system is designed together. Do not silently assume food restores health rather than energy, or that sleep is the only energy recovery method. The existing approved poker-table food/drink spending direction must be preserved while this model is resolved.
- Avoid punishing players for being offline, missing a real-world meal schedule or choosing to focus on social activities. The system should encourage believable routines without requiring real-time three-meals-a-day attendance.
- Exact meters, thresholds, decay rates, activity costs, meal timing, item effects, recovery rates, safe-state behavior and balancing remain TBD.

### Relationships, friends and social circles

The creator has directed that the social world support player relationships and a broader social network, including:
- A player may have a **girlfriend or boyfriend / romantic partner** and a supported in-game love-life/relationship experience.
- A persistent **friends list** for player connections.
- Distinct social-circle concepts such as **close homies** and **associates**, rather than treating every contact as the same relationship level.
- **Crews** remain an established group/social structure and should connect with the friends and social-circle systems without being conflated with an individual friendship or romantic relationship.

These are required social-world directions. The exact relationship progression, mutual-consent/acceptance flow, privacy and visibility settings, breakup/block/report tools, relationship status display, group permissions, benefits (if any), NPC relationship scope, and moderation/safety rules remain to be designed. Do not invent romance bonuses, exclusivity rules, marriage, shared property or other relationship mechanics without creator approval.

The social architecture should support clear player control: relationship invitations and status changes should be consensual, reversible and protected by privacy/blocking controls. No player should be forced into a romantic or social interaction.


## Creator clarification — energy recovery, offline behavior and expanded romance

### Energy, sitting activities and alcohol/smoking indicators

The creator has clarified the intended player-condition behavior:

- **Restorative seated activities:** Sitting at a bar and sitting while trading cards are approved activities that can restore player energy. The character should be able to recover while participating in these social activities rather than being required to stop having fun and enter a separate maintenance routine.
- **Smoking:** Smoking does not restore energy and does not decrease energy.
- **Drinking alcohol:** Drinking does not decrease energy. Alcohol consumption may instead be reflected through a separate visible alcohol-level/intoxication indicator. Do not treat that indicator as an energy meter.
- **No offline energy drain:** Energy must not deplete while the player is logged out. Do not impose offline decay or require players to log in multiple times per real-world day to maintain their character.
- **Return to play:** The creator's direction is that energy should come back when the player leaves/quits a session and returns, rather than carrying a depleted energy state forward as an offline penalty. The exact reset/recovery trigger, timing and whether recovery is immediate or uses a short transition remain technical details to specify; preserve the intended result of no persistent offline depletion.
- **Meter separation:** Keep energy distinct from alcohol level and do not make smoking or drinking an energy penalty. The broader distinction among health, energy/stamina and hunger/nutrition remains to be designed.

### Romantic relationships with players and NPCs

The creator has approved romantic relationship possibilities with **both real players and NPC characters**. The player may pursue a girlfriend/boyfriend or other supported romantic connection with either kind of character, based on who they find attractive and the interactions they choose.

For player-to-player relationships, the intended social value includes spending time together in Hustle City, talking and doing activities together, building a relationship through shared experiences, and potentially choosing to continue the connection outside the game. Any real-world connection, contact exchange or meeting must remain voluntary and mutual, with appropriate privacy, consent, blocking and safety controls. The game should facilitate social connection without exposing private information or pressuring players to move interactions off-platform.

NPC romance is also in scope as a relationship/roleplay possibility, with the exact NPC character eligibility, dialogue, progression, boundaries and gameplay depth still to be designed. Do not assume romance rewards, exclusivity, marriage, shared assets or other unapproved mechanics.

These clarifications are creator-approved product direction. Exact recovery rules, meter implementation, alcohol presentation, romance UX, consent and safety flows, moderation, NPC scope and technical implementation remain to be specified.

## Creator direction — unified player levels, stars and card acquisition

### One overall Hustle City progression level

The overall progression identity should connect the player's full journey through the game, with a visible star system as part of the level presentation/progression.

- The level should represent the player's overall journey, with poker remaining the central game motivation. How XP or progression credit is earned across poker, daily sessions, achievements, collections, social play, travel and other activities remains to be designed. Do not create separate competing player levels unless the creator later approves them.
- Progression must be long-term and rewarding without relying on arbitrary grind, pay-to-win advantages or punitive attendance requirements. No XP values, level curves, reward quantities or tier thresholds are approved yet.

### Card acquisition sources and controlled distribution

The creator's current direction is that cards should come primarily from active participation and achievement, with three approved acquisition channels:

1. **Poker hands and achievements:** Players can earn cards by winning hands, reaching qualifying win streaks, achieving hand-specific milestones or completing other poker/casino achievements. The broader direction is that qualifying casino play can produce card rewards, not only one specific poker table mode. Exact eligible games, hand conditions, achievement thresholds and reward quantities remain TBD.
2. **Daily sessions:** Participating in a daily play session can provide card-earning opportunities or rewards. This must not require logging in multiple times per day, penalize offline time or contradict the approved rule that energy does not drain while logged out. Exact cadence, eligibility and quantities remain TBD.
3. **Player-to-player trading:** Players should be able to trade cards with one another. Trading is a way to complete collections and exchange duplicates, not a source that creates additional cards in the overall economy.

The creator gave examples such as earning two cards after a fifth win or after a three-win streak. These are illustrative balancing ideas only; they do not establish approved thresholds, guaranteed drops or fixed quantities.

### Duplicate-aware collection economy

Card distribution should be randomized and controlled so that players do not repeatedly receive the same cards without meaningful progress. Duplicates are expected to occur and can create a reason for player-to-player trading. The system should support collection completion while preserving scarcity and long-term interest.

To be designed before balancing:
- Drop tables and rarity distribution by game, hand achievement and session participation.
- Whether a reward is guaranteed at a milestone or uses a probability.
- Duplicate handling, duplicate protection and collection-progress safeguards.
- Trade eligibility, confirmation, anti-scam protections, trade limits/cooldowns if needed, and transaction history.
- Whether cards are bound to a city, collection, player account or other progression context.
- How card acquisition relates to the established five-card sets, city collections and unlock rewards.
- Anti-farming, collusion and multi-account protections, especially for casino outcomes and repeated achievement triggers.

The card economy should be controlled through verified server-side game events and an auditable reward ledger. A player should not receive a card merely because a client reports a win; the authoritative game result must validate the reward. Trading transfers an existing card between players and must not duplicate it.

### Relationship between level progression and card progression

The unified player level and collectible card system are related but distinct progression tracks:
- The overall player level/stars represent the player's broad Hustle City journey.
- Cards are collectible rewards earned through poker/casino achievements, daily sessions and trades.
- Completed card sets/collections can unlock specified additional content or opportunities, subject to the existing launch-scope rule that creator-mandated launch destinations are playable from the start.
- The final design should make these systems reinforce one another without making card drops the only way to level up or making level advancement a substitute for completing collections.

The creator's stated core loop remains poker and casino participation, earning and trading cards, completing collections, unlocking additional content, traveling between cities and building a social lifestyle. A full job/employment system is not approved as a required progression pillar; keep it optional/unresolved unless the creator later directs otherwise.

All final level caps, rank names, star rules, XP sources and formulas, thresholds, reward tables, drop probabilities, daily-session definitions, card rarity, set requirements, trade rules and progression pacing require further design and creator approval.
