# Per-City Story Pack Template

## Purpose

Every playable city receives its own complete local story package. The Hustle City story bible is the flagship reference, not a one-time template that replaces the stories of other cities.

**Each city gets twelve district storylines**, one for each of its twelve districts. Each district storyline is adapted to that city's geography, economy, culture, businesses, local power structure, characters and events. Do not copy the same missions with only the city name changed.

## Required package for every city

1. City story overview and local identity.
2. Twelve district identities and twelve district storyline arcs.
3. City-level power structure and local leaders.
4. Six-card character progression for each configured district/business storyline.
5. Business-level and district-level financial outcome definitions.
6. Local supporting cast, relationships, rivalries, allies and unreliable contacts.
7. District-specific ambient conversations, phone calls, rumors, approaches, invitations and private meetings.
8. Local news, social feeds and public reactions.
9. City-specific discoveries and Afterplay Library records.
10. Connections to the wider Inner Circle / Ghost Network narrative where appropriate.
11. City-specific City Key and major progression milestone hooks, using the approved city progression architecture.
12. QA coverage for story continuity, player knowledge, mission branches and NPC knowledge boundaries.

## Local story versus global story

Each city must feel like it has its own life and conflicts. Not every business problem is caused by the Ghost Freight, The Twelve, the Inner Circle or the Ghost Network. Some stories are local, personal, economic, political, social or entirely independent.

Global organizations can create connective tissue across cities, but their presence must be discovered gradually through NPC relationships, ambient information, evidence and recurring patterns. Do not make every district mission a conspiracy mission.

The Ghost Network's own city and its twelve district leaders form a mirrored power structure. This mirror is a story device and should not be revealed prematurely. Other playable cities can have their own local power arrangements and unique histories.

## Per-city variation requirements

For each city, vary:
- District and neighborhood identities.
- Local district leaders and supporting characters.
- Business types, venue names and economic pressures.
- The central problem and canonical responsible person for each local arc.
- Character relationships, rivalries, alliances and social networks.
- Public spaces, event culture, schedules and ambient conversation style.
- Local clues, rumors and misinformation.
- Mission choices, costs, rewards and long-term consequences.

Character names, ages, nicknames, personalities and alignments can be authored autonomously. Major organization names, district names, building names and changes to overall strategy require creator approval.

## Authoring checklist

A city story pack is not complete when it has twelve short synopses. Each of the twelve district arcs must eventually contain:
- Six primary character roles and handoffs.
- Playable objectives and optional activities for all six chapters.
- Supporting NPCs and alternative routes.
- Sample dialogue and meaningful player choices.
- District-fund allocation and business/district financial consequences.
- Relationship, reputation and access consequences.
- Ambient discovery content independent of mission completion.
- A canonical authoring truth and bounded NPC knowledge.
- QA scenarios for both the mission layer and the ambient story layer.

Track city pack completeness by district and deliverable type. Mark outlines, scripted content, data-authored content, implemented content and verified content separately.
