# Hustle City — 30-City Food & Beverage Catalog

**Status:** Working catalog foundation, selected at the creator's direction. The 30-market roster below is the initial planning roster, not an irreversible geography decision. City-specific menu themes are starting identities and examples, not exhaustive or stereotype-based menus. Exact item lists, portions, recipes, prices and gameplay effects require further design and creator approval.

## 1. Core design direction

Hustle City has 30 starter city markets across the United States. Each market should have food and beverage choices that reflect recognizable local and regional food cultures. Players should notice meaningful differences when they travel between markets.

**Different menus, comparable affordability:** Cities should have distinct local menus, but the overall economy should use shared price tiers and comparable affordability targets. A player should not face a wildly different cost of basic participation simply because they traveled to another city. Small justified differences may be used for specialty items, portion sizes, venue class or premium presentation.

The flagship fictional Hustle City menu can combine a broad range of American and international influences, including soul food bowls, jerk chicken, shrimp dinners, rib tip dinners and seafood. Do not treat one city's menu as a substitute for every other city's identity.

## 4. Creator-provided price anchors

The creator wants in-game menu pricing to feel recognizable and grounded in everyday real-world prices. These are **reference anchors**, not a finalized price list:

| Item / category | Initial reference |
|---|---:|
| Basic meal entry point | Around $5.00 |
| Hamburger and fries example | $5.99 |
| Pop / soda | Around $2.00–$3.00 |
| Inexpensive liquor serving/example | Around $5.00 |
| Bottled liquor | Higher price based on bottle size and product tier |
| Premium / more expensive liquor | Higher price tier |

The final in-game catalog must define the currency symbol/label consistently as Hustle Money. These reference values do not establish a universal price for every city or venue. Verify the intended real-world price feel during economy balancing; do not silently change creator-provided anchors.

## 5. Shared menu taxonomy

Every city menu should be assembled from reusable catalog categories, with local variations:

- Meals and entrees.
- Affordable/basic meals.
- Signature/local meals.
- Premium meals and platters.
- Sides and add-ons.
- Snacks and desserts.
- Non-alcoholic drinks: water, pop/soda, juice, coffee, tea and specialty drinks.
- Alcoholic beverages, if included in the game: individual servings, beer/wine/liquor categories and bottles, with clear size/brand tiers.
- Venue-specific specials and limited-time local items.

Food and drinks are purchased with **Hustle Money**, never Poker Chips. Poker Chips remain a separate poker currency. Maintain separate wallets for Hustle Money, Diamonds, Poker Chips and Wings.

## 6. Price architecture and balancing rules

Build a shared price-tier framework first, then map local menu items to it:

1. **Entry/basic:** affordable everyday items and simple meals.
2. **Standard:** regular meals, combos and common drinks.
3. **Signature:** city-specific dishes and larger portions.
4. **Premium:** specialty meals, premium ingredients and upscale venue offerings.
5. **Luxury/rare:** high-end bottle service, exclusive venue menus and special-event offerings, where approved.

Each catalog item eventually needs:
- Stable item ID and display name.
- City/market availability and venue type.
- Category and local identity tags.
- Portion/serving/bottle size.
- Hustle Money price.
- Ingredient/quality or presentation tier where useful.
- Health restoration value, if food restores health.
- Energy effect, only if the creator approves food/drink energy effects.
- Availability schedule or limited-time status, if applicable.
- Age-rating/content classification where relevant to fictional alcohol depiction.
- Source notes and balancing status.

Do not set final health/energy values, portion sizes, exact liquor bottle prices, premium brand prices or complete city menus without further design and creator approval.

## 7. Economy algorithm dependencies

Daily rewards must not be finalized until the food and beverage price catalog and other major spending categories are sufficiently defined.

The balancing model should estimate:
- Basic food and drink costs for a typical play session/day.
- Spending differences between casual, regular and high-activity players.
- Venue class and city-market price differences.
- Expected frequency of buying meals and drinks.
- Food's approved relationship to health and energy systems.
- Savings goals for clothing and other discretionary purchases.
- Gameplay income, free daily grants, memberships and optional purchases.
- 7-, 30- and 90-day currency balances and time-to-afford targets.

Use a shared economy model to keep comparable menu tiers affordable across all 30 markets. The algorithm should initially recommend prices and reward levels for review; it must not dynamically change live prices or payouts without explicit approval and controlled economy testing.

## 9. Design guardrails

- Local food identity should be researched and represented respectfully; do not reduce a city or its residents to stereotypes.
- Keep the menu broad enough to represent the diversity within each market.
- Reuse common item data and price tiers where practical, while allowing city-specific names, recipes, variants and availability.
- Keep all food, drink and clothing prices in the Hustle Money economy unless a separate approved design says otherwise.
- Do not use Poker Chips to buy food or drinks.
- Do not finalize daily reward amounts before catalog and spending assumptions are modeled.
- Keep proposed items and prices clearly distinguished from creator-approved canon.
